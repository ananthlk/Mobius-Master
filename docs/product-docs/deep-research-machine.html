Deep Research — the machine

A caller asks for a fact, says how to judge it, what shape to return it in, and when they need it. The loop spends turns until the answer survives every check — or until it can name exactly what is stopping it and hand that to someone who can fix it.

Everything turns on one distinction. When a turn fails, the repair is either inside the loop or outside it, and that single fact decides whether another turn is worth spending.

Inside — spend another turn

The corpus can already answer this; the ask was wrong. Reframe and go again. Bounded by the turn budget, because that is what a budget is for.

refine · the loop fixes itself

Outside — file it and wait

The document is missing, unchunked, unindexed, or unreachable by the words in the question. No rephrasing fixes any of those. Spending turns here is waste, so the loop stops and files a repair.

acquire · reingest · reindex · lexicon — pollers

Up — raise a hand

Nothing mechanical is left: the source says nothing, two authorities disagree, or the clock ran out. Available from every state, not just the end.

registry · escalate — a person

How a repair gets done

The loop never performs a repair itself — it files one and stops. Pollers claim work they own, act on the world, and report back. A repair that lands wakes the turn that was waiting for it; a repair that cannot be done raises a hand instead of re-queueing forever.

sequenceDiagram
  participant L as Loop
  participant Q as research.diagnosis
  participant P as Poller
  participant W as World

  L->>Q: file repair (class, action, target, evidence, due_at)
  Note over L: turn -> waiting. No further turns spent.
  P->>Q: claim oldest unclaimed repair it owns
  Q-->>P: repair + target
  P->>W: fetch / re-chunk / re-index / add alias
  W-->>P: outcome
  P->>Q: resolve(outcome) or fail(reason, retryable)
  Q-->>L: repair settled
  L->>L: resume the turn that filed it

Two clocks, and they disagree on purpose

The turn budget bounds how much thinking a question is worth. The deadline bounds how long the caller can wait. They are not the same limit and neither implies the other: a request can exhaust three rounds in four minutes, or spend one round and then sit for two days behind an acquisition. Whichever runs out first ends the wait — the turn budget by escalating, the deadline by returning whatever survived, clearly marked as partial.

Three calls I need from you

What does “with confidence” mean? Today it is all-or-nothing: every required field survives, or the record is refused. The alternative is a caller-set bar — “three of five, flag the rest” — which returns more, and returns some facts that are only partly grounded. For a certified store I lean to all-or-nothing, with partials returned only at deadline and marked.
Who owns each repair class? acquire and reingest look like the RAG agent. reindex and lexicon look like Curation. If that is right, the poller contract is two consumers, not one, and the queue needs an owner column before either can claim work.
What happens at the deadline mid-repair? A document is being acquired and Tuesday arrives. Return nothing and say why, or return the partially-grounded answer marked as such? This is the caller's risk to take, which is why it sits in the request rather than in our defaults.
State names match research.request.status and research.turn.status. Marked “built” means the column or table exists and is written by the running loop — not that it has been exercised end to end.
