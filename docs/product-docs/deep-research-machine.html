Deep Research — the machine

A caller asks for a fact, says how to judge it, what shape to return it in, and when they need it. The loop spends turns until the answer survives every check — or until it can name exactly what is stopping it and hand that to someone who can fix it.

Everything turns on one distinction. When a turn fails, the repair is either inside the loop or outside it, and that single fact decides whether another turn is worth spending.

Inside — spend another turn

The corpus can already answer this; the ask was wrong. Reframe and go again. Bounded by the turn budget, because that is what a budget is for.

refine · the loop fixes itself

Outside — file it and wait

The document is missing, unchunked, unindexed, or unreachable by the words in the question. No rephrasing fixes any of those. Spending turns here is waste, so the loop stops and files a repair.

acquire · reingest · reindex · lexicon — pollers

Up — raise a hand

Nothing mechanical is left: the source says nothing, two authorities disagree, or the clock ran out. Available from every state, not just the end.

registry · escalate — a person

What the caller supplies

The bar is the caller's call, not ours. The registry wants a certified fact and takes all-or-nothing; Appeals wants a usable answer and will decide on confidence. Same machine, same states — one line of policy apart.

completeness

Every required field survived every check. Nothing partial is ever certified. Optional fields are captured and never block.

fact stores — service line registry

confidence

The weakest required field clears the caller's threshold. A record can be usable without being complete, and the caller says where that line is.

reasoning agents — Appeals

What comes back — always

One shape, at every stage, whatever the verdict. A caller polling a request that is still waiting on an acquisition gets the same envelope as one that succeeded — fewer fields filled in, and blocked_on saying what is holding it. Whatever was captured is returned even when the bar was not met, because a partial answer the caller can see is worth more than a failure they cannot inspect.

Two numbers, and they answer different questions

Confidence is the minimum across required fields, not the average — a record is only as sound as its weakest grounded value, and averaging lets four solid fields carry one invented one. Coverage is how much of required plus optional we hold. A record can be fully covered and barely confident, or highly confident about very little; collapsing them into one score would hide exactly the case a caller needs to see.

The judge — a bounded ReAct, not a chat

Yes: a single static call is too weak for the questions that decide a fact. But a judge with general reasoning and open tools is worse than a static call, because it can reason its way to “close enough” — the exact failure the extractor/critic split exists to prevent. The resolution is to constrain three things and leave the loop free inside them.

Three constraints that keep it a judge

Gates run first, outside the loop

The mechanical checks are strings and set membership — free, certain, and not arguable. A field that fails one never reaches the model, so there is no opportunity to reason past a quote that simply is not there.

Tools return evidence, never verdicts

A tool may say 59G-4.310 supersedes 59G-4.199 as of 2019-03-01. No tool says “this field is fine.” The judge alone concludes, and only into a closed vocabulary of issue classes.

Refute, do not verify

The loop’s job is to find a reason to reject, and it may only reach for tools that could produce one. “Verify this” makes a model agreeable; “break this” makes it useful.

The tools it gets

ToolReturnsWhy it earns a place
read_sourceopen the cited document at the cited pagecloses the hole. Today the quote is checked against chat’s answer, so a sentence chat invented and the extractor copied faithfully passes every check. Nothing currently reads the PDF.

rule_lookupsupersession, effective dates, authority tier59G-4.199 against 59G-4.310 was a real conflict this loop hit and could not settle. A lookup settles it; a model guesses.
code_lookupdoes this code belong to this line at allH2019/HR is a different service under Behavior Analysis than under CBH. Same code, different limits — the cross-line trap.
prior_factswhat the store already holds for this subjectA new fact contradicting a certified one is the most expensive failure here, and nothing checks for it today.
schema_diffwhat is missing against required and optionalDeterministic, and it is what populates coverage rather than asking a model to count.

What it does not get: web search, chat, or the extractor. Every one of those can introduce a new claim, and a judge that can introduce claims is a second extractor with a veto. The tool list is closed by that rule, not by count — anything that only checks an existing claim is a candidate; anything that can produce one is not.

What it costs, and how that is paid

A ReAct per field is far more expensive than one call per field. Three things keep it bounded: the gates reject most bad fields for free before any model runs; the budget is per field, so one hard value cannot consume what the easy ones needed; and verdicts cache on (value, quote, document), which recurs constantly because the same sentence grounds the same value across runs.

One more thing changes with this: the judge must draw its model independently of the extractor. They share a wrapper today, so a bad draw can produce both the claim and its approval.

Two poller consumers, not one — which is why the queue needs an owner column before either can claim work without racing the other.

How a repair gets done

The loop never performs a repair itself — it files one and stops. Pollers claim work they own, act on the world, and report back. A repair that lands wakes the turn that was waiting for it; a repair that cannot be done raises a hand instead of re-queueing forever.

sequenceDiagram
  participant L as Loop
  participant Q as research.diagnosis
  participant P as Poller
  participant W as World

  L->>Q: file repair (class, action, target, evidence, due_at)
  Note over L: turn -> waiting. No further turns spent.
  P->>Q: claim oldest unclaimed repair it owns
  Q-->>P: repair + target
  P->>W: fetch / re-chunk / re-index / add alias
  W-->>P: outcome
  P->>Q: resolve(outcome) or fail(reason, retryable)
  Q-->>L: repair settled
  L->>L: resume the turn that filed it

Two clocks, and they disagree on purpose

The turn budget bounds how much thinking a question is worth. The deadline bounds how long the caller can wait. They are not the same limit and neither implies the other: a request can exhaust three rounds in four minutes, or spend one round and then sit for two days behind an acquisition. Whichever runs out first ends the wait — the turn budget by escalating, the deadline by returning whatever survived, clearly marked as partial.

Settled, and what it costs

All-or-nothing for fact stores; confidence for everyone else. Both are the same machine with one line of policy different, so a second consumer is a config change and not a fork. The cost is that confidence has to mean something defensible per field — which is why it is the minimum over required fields and is computed from the critic’s own per-field verdicts, not asserted separately.
RAG owns acquire and reingest; Curation owns reindex and lexicon. Two consumers claiming from one queue. Nothing prevents them racing today, so the owner column and the claim are the first thing to build, before either side is asked to poll.
Return whatever was captured, always, and mark it. At the deadline the caller gets the partial with partial_because: "deadline" — and the same envelope answers a mid-flight poll. The risk this creates is a partial being mistaken for a finished answer downstream, so verdict and partial_because are top-level, not buried in metadata.
