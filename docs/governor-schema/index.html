Governor — the SLA

The promise is quality, latency and cost. Rounds are how we try to keep it.

"SLA IS NOT ABOUT NUMBER OF ROUNDS THATS WRONG. THE SLA IS THE PROMISE OF QUALITY (recall and accuracy), LATENCY AND COST" — Ananth, 2026-09-10

Precedence: caller > user > default. A declared term is never widened by the system — only narrowed, and only by a hard limit: the infra deadline, or a compliance gate.

5 · What the governor does with it

phasereadsdecides
A · scope
once, before round 1		the SLAthe levers: rounds, extensions, depth, model, how many tools, whether the floors run
B · spend
every round		progress against the SLAcontinue · buy a round · consolidate · finalize — and which term is at risk
C · hand off
once, at the end		what was actually achievedtell the integrator which terms were met and which were not, so the answer can say so
This is what makes Phase C matter. If the SLA is three terms, the hand-off is three answers: recall met, accuracy met, latency exceeded. An answer that ran short on accuracy should say so — which is exactly what the CARC 197 turn failed to do when it stated a 90-day deadline its own disclaimer called unsupported.

There is no normal depth in production. _DEPTH_RANK defines fast / normal / thinking; only fast and thinking ever appear across 1,120 turns. The middle rung is declared and unreachable.
In copilot, thinking is not slower than fast — it is marginally faster. p50 31.3s vs 31.5s, p90 50.9s vs 56.5s, on the same 2.4–2.5 rounds. Depth costs 11% more tokens and buys no measurable extra work. The dial does nothing in copilot. In agentic it plainly does: 126.9s vs 91.8s and 2.3× the cost.
The latency promise no mode can keep is agentic. Its p90 is 211s (fast) and 275s (thinking) against a 300s hard ceiling — so the ceiling binds on roughly a tenth of agentic turns, and soft_target_s = 120s is already past p50. The governor is consolidating on more than half of agentic turns before the round budget matters.
Cost has an order-of-magnitude spread and no term to bound it. agentic/thinking p90 is 659,406 tokens against quick/fast p50 of 24,940 — 26×. And $0.1098 vs $0.0102 per turn at p50, 10.8×. Nothing in the contract caps either.

Read this as a floor, not an accuracy figure. 96–98% is the pass rate of a gate that only runs when the planner already proposes completion at a medium/high bar — it is conditioned on the turn believing it is done. It says nothing about recall, and in quick it never runs at all, so quick has no accuracy signal whatsoever. Zero turns finalized on budget exhaustion in 30 days, which means the round budget is not the binding constraint anywhere.
The mapping error, precisely. confidence_bar (low / medium / high) is the closest thing to a quality term, and it is a threshold on the planner's self-report, gated by a groundedness check. It promises neither recall nor accuracy — it promises that a model said it was confident and a check did not object.
